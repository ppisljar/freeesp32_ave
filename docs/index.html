An open audio-visual entrainment device you can actually inspect

freeesp32_ave is ESP-IDF firmware that turns an off-the-shelf ESP32 audio board into a mind-machine: it synthesises binaural beats and isochronic tones in real time, drives LED photic stimulation locked to the same timeline, and plays both from a plain-text session script you write yourself. It is a low-cost, hackable alternative to commercial AVE hardware — and unlike that hardware, every number below was measured on the bench rather than claimed on a datasheet.

Read before you build or use this

This device deliberately flashes light in the 3–30 Hz band. That band is a genuine seizure trigger for people with photosensitive epilepsy, and bright flicker around 15–25 Hz is the risk peak; saturated red is a special case of its own. Do not use it — and do not point it at anyone — if there is any history of epilepsy, seizures or photosensitivity in the user or their family, and stop immediately on any aura, nausea, disorientation or involuntary movement.

The firmware contains no safety interlock. A brightness/depth clamp for the high-risk band was designed and deliberately left unimplemented (logged as item B3 in reports/non_planned_reports/entrainment_firmware_report.md), so a session file can command any rate and brightness the hardware can produce. The authoring discipline is yours, not the device's.

This is a DIY research and meditation instrument built by hobbyists. It is not a certified medical device, it has no clinical validation, and nothing here is medical advice or a treatment claim.

What the device does

You write a session as a .ledc text file: one line per event, absolute timestamps in milliseconds, one column per parameter. The device parses it, then executes audio and light from the same clock. 

# t=0: 12 Hz binaural beat, amber flicker locked to it on every zone
0         12    70  60  255 50 0  255
A 0       105  -100  60   0  1      0
A 0       117   100  60   0  2      0

# 10 minutes later: glide the beat down to 7.83 Hz
600000   >7.83  70  60  255 50 0  255
A 600000 >107.085 -100 60 0  1      0
A 600000 >114.915  100 60 0  2      0

Lines with no prefix drive light; A lines drive a synthesis channel; BG adds a continuous background track and S adds spoken cues. A > or * prefix turns a value into a linear or quadratic glide to the next entry, and five more prefixes turn it into a self-contained oscillation. The full column reference lives in the session developer guide.

Everything is reachable over Wi-Fi. The board joins your network (or raises its own ESP32-AVE-Setup access point if it cannot) and serves a web UI for uploading sessions, driving playback and changing every pin and backend at runtime. With a microSD card fitted, speech and background audio come off the card and a session runs with no browser attached at all.

Three entrainment modalities

Each one is a different physical route to the same idea — presenting the brain with a rhythm at a chosen frequency. The firmware implements all three, and a session can run them together on the same timeline.

Binaural beats

Two slightly detuned carriers, one per ear (freq left, freqR right). The beat is the difference, produced inside the listener rather than in the air, so it needs headphones. Per-channel carriers run to 20 kHz and the beat can be slowly dithered to fight habituation.

Isochronic tones

One carrier, gated on and off at the entrainment rate (mod) with a selectable envelope — square, sine, triangle, trapezoid or tremolo — plus duty, attack and depth. Works on speakers, and the gate rate measured 8.0107 Hz against a commanded 8.000.

Photic (LED) flicker

Light pulsed in front of closed eyes, up to 100 Hz, across eight logical zones with independent rate, duty, brightness, colour and phase. A 180° phase offset between two zones gives a luminance-flat "invisible" flicker — measured at 178.1° against a commanded 180.

Any entrainment field can also be a carrier for one of eight waveforms (sine, square, triangle, sawtooth, white/pink/brown noise, and a replicated EEG contour), and all of them were verified spectrally: the geometric waves match theory to within 0.001–0.004, pink noise measured −3.04 dB/octave against −3, brown −6.07 over 60 Hz–3 kHz against −6.

How a session reaches your senses

Timing, as measured

Entrainment is a timing claim, so the timing was measured rather than assumed. The rig: an Arturia MiniFuse 4 with all four inputs on one ADC clock (so inter-channel skew is zero), two solar cells watching the LEDs and the device's headphone output on the other two channels. Edges are located by the peak of the derivative inside each cycle, and all smoothing is zero-phase. Every figure below is measured on hardware, not a specification.

Why −2.7 ms and not zero

The residual offset is deliberately not calibrated away. It cannot currently be separated into a device-side offset and a bias in the measurement chain: the audio path adds the codec's interpolation-filter group delay and its analog output stage, the light path adds LED turn-on and the photocell's RC, and neither is independently known. Trimming the constant to zero would bake one particular measurement rig's bias into the firmware. For context, cross-modal audio/light simultaneity tolerance is roughly 20–40 ms, so a 2.7 ms constant is well inside imperceptible — the microsecond capability is margin, not a requirement.

What it is not

  • Not a medical device. No certification, no clinical trial, no therapeutic claim. Treat it as an instrument for self-directed experimentation.
  • No safety interlock. Nothing in the firmware limits brightness or modulation depth in the 15–25 Hz risk band. See the warning above.
  • Quieter than it should be. The mixer divides every channel by a fixed 1/16 regardless of how many are playing, which costs 24 dB that no session can recover: a sine at maximum volume on every control measured −27.1 dBFS at the device output. No shipped session uses more than 4 of the 16 channels, so the divisor is sized for a case that never occurs. The fix is understood and deliberately unapplied — it would make existing sessions up to 24 dB louder on a head-worn device, which is a decision a human has to take, not a patch.
  • Under active development. Known rough edges are written down rather than hidden, including an audio line that silently resets its waveform when a trailing column is omitted, and one LED carrier shape whose harmonic content still has an open measurement question.
