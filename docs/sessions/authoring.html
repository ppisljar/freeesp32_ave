Authoring guide

The session arc

Every shipped session is built from four phases, joined by > and * ramps:

PhaseWhat it doesTypical length
1. Onset / induction Start where the brain already is — around 10–14 Hz alpha — and hold. This is where light and volume fade in.1–3 min
2. Deepen Glide from the onset rate toward the target band, slope-limited.3–10 min
3. Hold Sit at the target. This is the core of the session and it should be the longest phase.≥5–6 min; 10–20 min for theta or delta
4. Return Ramp back toward alpha before the eyes open — unless the goal says otherwise.2–3 min

Starting at the target rate rather than gliding into it is the single most common structural mistake. The frequency-following response needs something to follow; dropping someone straight onto 2 Hz gives it nothing to track.

Pacing: ramp slopes and dwell times

Kind of sessionSlopeShipped example

Two practical notes. First, the 40 Hz gamma protocol is the exception to all of this: its effect is specified at a fixed rate, so there is no glide, no LFO and no jitter in 07_genus_40hz at all. Second, when you glide, remember you are gliding at least two things — the audio beat and the LED rate — and each needs its own > on its own line. There is no follow mechanism.

Safe ramp-in and ramp-out

These are the numbers the whole library observes, and the reason is as much anti-startle as it is entrainment:

  • Light: ramp in over at least 10 s, and in practice 15 s is the library's habit. Ramp out too — never stop a flicker abruptly.
  • Audio: ramp in over at least 5 s, and 30–60 s for a volume fade-in that should be unnoticeable.
  • Keep a brightness floor. A 50 % duty with a non-zero dim level avoids a full black-to-white contrast swing.
  • Fade a flicker out at a trough, not at a bright peak — it disengages more cleanly.
  • Cap brightness. The library's caps run from 12 % for sleep beds to 45 % for the full-field 40 Hz protocol. Below drive ~100 you are in the non-linear part of the drive curve, so a dim ramp is distorted in shape as well as shallow.

The canonical ramp-in idiom is three rows: a >0 at t=0, the target value at t=15000, and then whatever the session does next.

0     10 50 >0 255 160 32 15 1 0   # brightness ramps from 0 ...
15000 10 50 24 255 160 32 15 1 0   # ... to 24% over 15 s

Goal-specific endings

Do not use a generic return for these:

  • Sleep and lucid-dream sessions skip the return phase entirely and end in a soft off: brightness and volume fade to zero simultaneously at the low target rate, with no wake-up.
  • Focus and energise sessions end bright and alert: ramp up toward 12–15 Hz and brighten the light; never fade to dark, which leaves people groggy.
  • Meditation and relaxation return to alpha and then close calmly.

Whatever the ending, finish every channel explicitly at zero. The timeline does not tidy up after you, the jitter globals are not reset between sessions, and a ramp with no target entry never fires at all.

Anti-habituation

A dead-steady beat held for fifteen minutes habituates. Three mechanisms, and they are not interchangeable:

MechanismWritten asMovesScope
LED jitter0.3:53000 in LED column 11the flicker rateper mask; amp clamped to 5 Hz
Audio jitter0.3:53000 in audio column 12the binaural detune onlyglobal; a no-op on mono channels
Periodic modulation~9.7:10.3:53000 on mod or a carrierwhatever cell carries itper channel

Keep the amplitude small — ±0.1 to 0.3 Hz is the usual range — and use non-commensurate periods across layers (37 s and 53 s, not 30 s and 60 s) so they do not re-synchronise into an audible super-period. A period below 1000 ms is silently ignored on both sides.

There is also a separate, slower layer worth knowing about: a 0.1 Hz breath LFO (about six breaths a minute) on brightness or volume, independent of the entrainment rate, used for coherence pacing. 26_alpha_breath holds 10 Hz alpha with exactly that. Write it as a periodic modulation: ~15:35:10000. Keep it fixed per segment; an asymmetric inhale/exhale shape would need a new LFO shape in firmware and does not exist yet.

How to test a session

In order, cheapest first.

1. Lint it. No hardware needed.

python3 tools/avmeasure/analyze.py lint --ledc path/to/session.ledc

Exit code 0 means no errors or warnings; exit 1 means at least one. Infos do not fail the run. --keep-speech models a raw POST of the file including its S rows — by default they are stripped, because the browser strips them before POSTing and that is what the device actually receives. --json gives machine-readable output.

The linter is a model of the device, not a grammar check: it replays the timeline, tracks each channel's live state, and reports things that will behave differently from how the file reads. That is why it can catch a ramp pointing at a -.

2. Read it back as prose.

Every row of a good session carries an inline comment saying what changes and why. If you cannot write that comment, the row is probably wrong. Look especially for rows whose literal disagrees with the live state — the device will start a ramp from the live value and your file will have lied to the next reader.

3. Play it, with the serial monitor attached.

The device logs a one-line summary of every dispatched entry, including the resolved sweep (start → target over duration), plus a warning for every clamp and rejection. That log is the fastest way to confirm an entry executed as you intended, and the only way to see the clamps the linter does not model.

4. Measure it.

There is a complete measurement harness in tools/avmeasure/ and a feature-verification suite of fourteen short sessions in sessions/test/ (12 min 32 s total) whose README states, block by block, what every number should be and which instrument produces it. If you are changing firmware rather than authoring a session, start there.

Three things from that suite that matter even if you never run it:

  • Check capture integrity before believing any per-cycle number. A recorder can drop a block of frames mid-capture and still write a file of exactly the right length. Five such steps were found in one 665-second capture, and one of them was written up as a firmware defect and survived a day of scrutiny — because both optical sensors agreed on it. They agreed because the whole recording stepped, not because the LED did.
  • Differential measurements are immune to that; absolute per-cycle timing is not. Audio-versus-light offset, inter-eye offset and every harmonic ratio survive a timebase step. Single-cycle claims do not.
  • A disconnected sensor produces confident, fictional results. Run the signal check first. And always print the fundamental's absolute level in dBFS beside any harmonic ratio: a degraded signal path inflates even harmonics specifically, and the same session measured h2/h1 = 0.547 with a faulty sensor wire and 0.044 after it was repaired.

5. Validate against the library rules.

Sessions destined for sessions/library/ must also pass node validate_session.mjs library/<file>.ledc with zero errors, and must follow sessions/SESSION_DESIGN_GUIDELINES.md — bearing in mind the drift noted below.

The static linter, finding by finding

Every code the linter can emit, what it means, and what to do about it.

Lint findings. Severities are as emitted by tools/avmeasure/ledc_expect.py.
CodeSeverityWhat it meansWhat to do
LEDC_ENTRY_CAPerror More than 100 entries. Everything past the cap is silently dropped at parse. Split the session, or merge rows that share a timestamp and a channel.
LEDC_BATCH_CAPerror More than 50 entries share one timestamp. Only the first 50 execute, and the rest fire never, with no runtime log line at all. Spread the batch over a few milliseconds, or reduce it. This is the one device limit with no runtime diagnostic, so the lint is your only warning.
LEDC_AUDIO_CH_RANGEerror ch outside 0–15. It is clamped to channel 0, so the audio plays somewhere other than where the file says. Fix the channel number. Remember the range is 0-based: 16 is out of range.
LEDC_RAMP_TO_ZEROerror A >/* on a core field whose target entry writes - for that field. The target is read from the struct member with no present-bit check, so it is still 0 — the ramp slides to zero instead of holding. Write the explicit value on the target row.
LEDC_LED_REJECTEDerror An LED entry out of range on the no-sweep path: freq > 100, duty > 100 or bright > 100. It is dropped, not clamped — the channel keeps doing whatever it was doing. Bring the value into range.
LEDC_FREQR_PREFIXerror Now a false positive. It reports that a glyph on freqR parses as 0.0 and collapses the channel to mono. That was true and nasty; the parser now handles > and * on freqR correctly. Either accept the finding knowing it is stale, or use the equivalent carrier-ramp idiom — see gliding a binaural beat.
LEDC_AUDIO_CH8_NO_RAMPerror Historical: ramps on channels 8–15 used to be truncated to a step by a uint8_t bitmask. Fixed; the branch is effectively dead. Nothing. If you see it, something is odd.
LEDC_LINE_TRUNCATEDwarning A line exceeds 256 bytes and is truncated, which silently changes its token count and so its meaning. Shorten the line — usually the inline comment.
LEDC_LED_TOKENS / LEDC_AUDIO_TOKENSwarning An illegal token count: 6, 7 or more than 12 on an LED line; fewer than 5 on an audio line. The whole line is skipped. Count the columns. A missing trailing column is legal; a missing middle one is not — use -.
LEDC_LED_MASK_ZEROwarning mask 0 — the line is rejected at parse and the entry never exists. Pick a real mask. Note - in the mask column also parses as 0.
LEDC_AUDIO_GLUED_TIMEwarning A1500 … instead of A 1500 …. The glued token is discarded entirely and every field shifts left by one. Put a space after the A.
LEDC_LED_OUT_OF_RANGEwarning An out-of-range LED value on the sweep path. Historically this executed unchecked; the sweep path now carries the same guards and checks both ramp endpoints. Bring the value into range.
LEDC_DASH_FREQ_STOPSwarning - in the LED freq column on a channel with no live rate. It resolves to 0, which stops the flicker and paints the channel black. Write the rate explicitly in a channel's first entry.
LEDC_FIRST_BATCH_HOISTwarning The earliest entry is not at t=0, so the first batch fires at wall-clock 0 regardless of its timestamp. Benign for LED, not for audio. Start the file at t=0.
LEDC_END_STOP_SKIPPEDwarning The final timestamp is over the batch cap, so the end-of-timeline stop never runs — the LEDs flicker indefinitely. Reduce the final batch. This is a genuinely unsafe failure mode.
LEDC_LAST_ENTRY_CANCELLEDwarning A final entry is immediately overridden, so its intent never takes effect. Usually a duplicated or stale closing row — remove it.
LEDC_SPEECH_TIME / LEDC_SPEECH_UNQUOTEDwarning An S row with an unparsable time, or with text that is not quoted. Quote the text. The parser reads between the first and the last ".
LEDC_BG_BAD / LEDC_BG_SCHEMEwarning A malformed BG row, or a URL scheme that is not http(s)://, sdcard:// or push://. Fix the row. Remember BG is last-one-wins.
LEDC_MASK_HIGH_BITSinfo The mask selects channels that light nothing on the current channel map. Often deliberate — the measurement sessions park a keepalive channel on an unpopulated LED. Check your map and move on.
LEDC_ORPHAN_RAMPinfo A ramp with no later entry on that channel to supply a target, so it never fires. Add the target row, or drop the glyph.
LEDC_RAMP_START_SUBSTITUTEDinfo A ramp's literal start value differs from the channel's live value. The device will use the live value, so the file is misleading its reader. Write the live value. This is the single most useful info-level finding.
LEDC_AUDIO_DUTY_ZEROinfo A stepped audio duty 0, which is a no-op: the setter only writes duty > 0, so the previous duty stays. Use mod 0 to stop modulating, or vol 0 to silence.

A failing lint is not always wrong. sessions/test/t13_rejects.ledc exists to trip the linter — exactly 5 errors, 4 warnings and 2 infos, and its job is to prove the device diagnoses each fault rather than crashing on it. Likewise 30_splitfield_lucid deliberately drives one eye in the photosensitive band and expects warnings. What matters is that every finding is either fixed or explained in the file's header.

The traps

Collected because each of these has actually cost someone time.

Values that are silently clamped or wrapped

FieldWhat happensWarned?
LED attackCapped at 60 ms on the stepped path, and the renderer further clamps the effective edge to half the on-time. The ramped path does not cap the stored value, so a ramped attack can read above 60 while rendering identically to 60.no
LED duty, bright101–255 is caught by the guards and the entry is dropped. Above 255 the uint8_t cast wraps — 300 becomes 44 — and the guard then sees an in-range value.no
LED R G BClamped to 255.yes, log line naming the field
Audio chOut of range clamps to channel 0 — so the audio plays, just not where you said.yes
LED jitter ampClamped to 5 Hz.no
Both jitter periodsBelow 1000 ms, silently ignored — the previous period stays in force rather than the value you wrote.no
Audio duty, depthClamped to 0–100 at parse.no
Audio vol, pan, freqNot validated at all on any session path. vol 500 works and is ~14 dB louder; pan 200 inverts the left channel's polarity.no

Fields that particular envelopes ignore

  • duty is ignored by env 1 (sine) and env 2 (triangle), on both the audio and LED paths. Neither gate function reads it. A duty column next to them is a silent no-op, and so is a duty modulation — the modulation engine animates a value nothing reads. If duty matters, use env 0 or env 3.
  • attack is read only by env 3. On env 0, 1, 2 or 4 it does nothing. Write it as 0 there so the file does not imply an edge shaping that never happens.
  • env 4 has no meaning on an LED line and is treated as square.
  • Audio jitter only ever moves a binaural detune, so on a mono or isochronic channel it is a complete no-op — however confidently a session header says otherwise. 36_shamanic_trance_drum's header claims its jitter wanders the isochronic pulse rate; it does not. To wander a pulse rate, modulate mod.

A column whose omission used to reset a channel

Audio waveType. Omitting the column on a running channel used to silently reset it to sine — 21 occurrences across the shipped library — because the wave type was filled from the parsed (zero-initialised) value and latched unconditionally. The parser now reads the live waveform back when the column is absent, and the corresponding lint has been retired. If you are reading older measurements or an older session header that worries about this, it is fixed. The general rule now holds without exception: an absent trailing column means "leave unchanged".

freq 0 on an LED line turns the light OFF

It does not give you a steady lamp. frequency ≤ 0 stops the flicker on the mask and paints those channels black; the bright and RGB values on that row never reach the output. Several shipped sessions were authored on the opposite belief — most visibly 05_focus_smr, which holds freq 0 with a rising brightness for thirty minutes and comments "light STAYS ON and BRIGHTENS". It does not. There is no way to command a steady, non-flickering light from a .ledc timeline; an audio-only session simply has no LED line.

The accidental binaural beat

An omitted freqR means "keep the live right carrier", and a mono channel's live right carrier equals its left one. So a row that moves freq and omits freqR leaves the right ear at the old pitch, trips the binaural-bypass test, starts playing two different carriers, beats at the size of your carrier change, and silently stops honouring pan. Restate freqR — 0 for mono — on every audio row that touches freq. No lint catches it.

A restart re-latches env, attack and jitter

A fresh LED flicker start writes the carrier waveform (square), attack 3 and the jitter globals unconditionally — and the timeline treats a mask as needing a fresh start unless every bit in it is already active. So a row that restarts a channel after a freq 0 stop, or a multi-bit mask where one bit happens to be idle, resets those three columns to their defaults on every bit in the mask. The sweep path does not re-latch, so whether it happens depends on whether the row carries a ramp. Restate env on every row that restarts a channel. Not covered by a lint.

Depth defaults are envelope-dependent

Omitting both env and depth gives you 10 %; writing env 0..3 and omitting depth gives you 100 %; writing env 4 and omitting depth gives you 10 % again. That is deliberate and documented, and it is still the most surprising rule in the format. Write the column.

Ramp semantics

  • Animate-on-start. The glyph sits on the row where the ramp begins; the target comes from the next row on that channel. A ramp on the last row never fires.
  • A ramp on a running channel starts from the live value, not from the literal. Write the live value or the file lies.
  • A ramp pointing at a - ramps to zero for core fields. Pulse fields scan forward correctly; core fields do not.
  • A new entry pre-empts whatever animation was running on that channel's fields, then re-arms from the new row. That is how you stop an LFO: write a static value.

phase is a lead

Writing phase 90 makes the edge arrive a quarter period earlier, not later — measured 74.76 ms rather than 25 ms at 10 Hz. To delay by N degrees, write 360 − N. The whole shipped library only ever uses 0 and 180, which are symmetric under negation, which is why nobody has been bitten yet.

Audio channel numbering changes between the file and the API

.ledc ch is 0-based; /api/state reports audio channels 1-based, so ch 8 reads back there as ch9. Convert once and write it down.

Where the docs have drifted from the code

Three places in the repo's own documents state things the current firmware no longer does. They are listed here because a reader who trusts them will author the wrong file.

DocumentSaysThe code does
ledc_format.md v3, “depth” “depth 0 does NOT currently mean no modulation… fixing this needs a negative unset sentinel in the generator.” That sentinel now exists. iso_depth initialises to -1.0f and the test is >= 0.0f, so a written depth 0 is honoured and removes the modulation. The spec's warning is historical.
sessions/SESSION_DESIGN_GUIDELINES.md §7 “LED freq 0 = steady-on (no flicker)”, and §11 describes 05_focus_smr's steady lamp built on it. freq ≤ 0 stops the flicker and goes dark. This one has shipped consequences — see the trap above.
sessions/SESSION_DESIGN_GUIDELINES.md §3, §7, §8 and many session headers “freq_R is NOT interpolatable”; “env, phase, attack are step-only on-device… don't put >/ */~ on them”; “AM depth is hard-fixed at ~0.1”. All three are now out of date. freqR and depth take > and *; duty, phase and attack are fully compound; depth is a real column. env remains step-only — that part is still true.

And one place the tooling has drifted: the linter still raises LEDC_FREQR_PREFIX at error severity for a glyph on freqR, which the device now handles correctly. The browser-side parser is also behind the device on both v3 audio changes — its freqR is a bare float parse and it has no depth column — so a session using either will behave differently in the browser preview than on hardware.

When in doubt, ledc_format.md outranks every other document in the repo, and main/config_parser.c outranks ledc_format.md.

Checklist before you ship

  • Lints clean, or every finding is deliberate and explained in the header.
  • Starts at t=0; every channel ends explicitly at zero; jitter written back to 0.
  • Follows the four-phase arc with the correct goal-specific ending; hold dwell at least 5–6 minutes.
  • Ramp slopes within the working range; light fades in and out over at least 10 s; audio over at least 5 s.
  • No bright full-field flicker at 15–25 Hz. If the target rate is in that band, the audio carries the beat and there is no LED line.
  • Brightness capped, with a floor. No saturated-red flicker above 3 Hz, no red↔blue alternation at flicker rates.
  • Eyes-closed sessions biased amber or red at 620 nm or longer.
  • Binaural: carriers below ~1 kHz, beat below ~35 Hz, headphones stated in the header, and freqR restated on every row that moves a carrier.
  • Isochronic: depth written explicitly, vol raised to pay for it, env 3 with a few ms of attack unless a click is the intent.
  • Monaural: both halves pan 0, freqR 0, matched vol, same timestamp.
  • Under 100 entries, under 50 per timestamp, every line under 256 bytes.
  • A header comment block with the name, goal, duration, bands, the techniques used and why, an honest note on the evidence, and the full safety and contraindication block.
