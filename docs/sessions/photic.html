Photic / LED flicker

Flickering light at the target rate. By a wide margin the strongest driver this device has — and the only one that can hurt someone. Everything on this page is written with that asymmetry in mind.

Photosensitivity and epilepsy. Flickering light between roughly 3 and 30 Hz can provoke a seizure in people with photosensitive epilepsy. The risk peaks in the 15–25 Hz band, around 16–20 Hz, which accounts for the great majority of photosensitive reactions. Saturated red flicker is a special-risk case and should be kept at or below 3 Hz regardless of brightness. Red↔blue alternation at flicker rates is also specifically hazardous.

There is no device-side safety clamp. The firmware does not limit brightness or depth in the danger band — that interlock was cut, and the only automated guard is a one-time browser-side opt-in. Safety is entirely the author's responsibility, expressed in the timeline by hand.

Do not use the device, and do not hand anyone a session, if there is any history of photosensitive epilepsy, a seizure disorder, or photic migraine. Never use it while driving or operating machinery. Stop if you feel dizzy or unwell. Read the safety section on the overview page. This is a DIY research instrument, not a medical device.

What the phenomenon is

Present a light that oscillates at a fixed rate and the visual cortex produces an oscillation at the same rate, measurable at the scalp. That response — the steady-state visually evoked potential, or SSVEP — is one of the most robust and best-characterised signals in electrophysiology. It is large, it is reproducible across subjects, it tracks the stimulus rate across a wide range, and it has harmonics at integer multiples of the drive rate. It is used routinely as a measurement tool precisely because it is so dependable.

With the eyes closed — which is how almost every session here is used — the eyelid is doing the filtering. It transmits roughly 14.5 % at 700 nm but 3 % or less below 580 nm, so for closed-eye work only warm light arrives at all. That is why the eyes-closed sessions in the shipped library are biased amber and red.

Three cycles of a flicker gate. The upper trace is duty 30 at phase 0: the light is on for 30 % of each period. The lower trace is the same gate written with phase 90, and its rising edge sits a quarter of a period earlier, not later — see phase is a lead below. Flicker rate, duty and phase are the three time-domain controls; brightness and colour are the amplitude controls; env sets the shape of the edge between them.

Mechanism, as currently understood

Two claims need separating, because one is strong and one is not.

Strong: that periodic light produces a cortical oscillation at the stimulus rate. That is the SSVEP and it is not in doubt. Supporting details are also well measured — flicker drives harder at the spectral extremes, so amber/red and blue produce substantially larger responses than green at matched luminance (one dataset puts amber/red around 8 dB and blue/cyan around 6.8 dB above a green/lime reference, with green/lime itself only 2.85 dB above baseline); combined audio-plus-visual stimulation at the same rate produces a larger steady-state response than either modality alone; and the response takes a few hundred milliseconds to stabilise after onset, which is why single-cycle timing jitter is irrelevant and millisecond-scale sync is already overkill.

Not strong: that producing an SSVEP at rate X installs the subjective state that convention associates with band X. The band-to-state table is practitioner convention. The one protocol with substantial clinical investment behind it is the 40 Hz gamma work, and even there the split is clear: the entrainment itself is well established, while the disease-modifying claims built on top of it are contested and the human evidence is early. Treat photic sessions as experiential. The shipped library's headers do, explicitly, and yours should.

The closed-eye imagery effects — the geometric form constants, the moving patterns people report under steady flicker around 10 Hz, sometimes called Ganzflicker — are a real and reliably reproducible perceptual phenomenon, independent of any entrainment claim. They are a perfectly good reason to build a session.

What it is typically used for

  • Any session where you want the strongest available drive, locked 1:1 to an audio beat. Combined is better than either alone.
  • The 40 Hz gamma protocol. 40 Hz is above the photosensitive danger band, which is what makes a full-field bright version defensible at all. 07_genus_40hz.ledc is the faithful transcription; 27_genus_40hz_dim.ledc is the comfort variant built on the antiphase pair.
  • Closed-eye imagery work. 15_ganzflicker_imagery.ledc holds 10 Hz with a sine gate and a slow ±0.5 Hz float; 31_ganzfeld_amber.ledc is a steady amber Ganzfeld field.
  • Split-field work — a different rate to each eye. 30_splitfield_lucid.ledc drives the left eye at 16 Hz and the right at 6 Hz via two different masks.
  • Mood, pacing and circadian framing, via colour and a slow brightness breath, rather than via the flicker rate at all.

On this firmware that session runs dark. The format spec is unambiguous — “<= 0 means STOP FLICKER on the mask, not DC-on” — and the code agrees with the spec.

So: there is no way to command a steady, non-flickering light from a .ledc timeline. An audio-only session simply has no LED line in it. If you need the beat in the 15–25 Hz danger band, that is the correct shape for the file anyway.

Zone masks

mask is a bit field over eight logical LED channels: bit 0 (value 1) is LED1, bit 1 (value 2) is LED2, and so on up to bit 7 (value 128). OR the bits to address several at once, so 15 is LED1–LED4 and 3 is LED1+LED2. A mask of 0 causes the entire line to be rejected at parse with a log line, so the entry never exists.

Which physical pixels a mask lights is a build-time setting, not a format fact. CONFIG_LED_CHANNEL_MAP is a flat comma-separated list, one channel index per pixel, so the same mask means different things on different builds. Session headers in the library therefore ship a channel-map recipe alongside the masks they use. A common glasses layout assigns ch0 = left-cool, ch1 = left-warm, ch2 = right-cool, ch3 = right-warm, which gives you:

  • mask 3 — the whole left eye; mask 12 — the whole right eye; mask 15 — both
  • mask 10 — the warm bank of both eyes; mask 5 — the cool bank of both eyes (this is the pairing the antiphase trick uses)

Masks 9 and 6 look like an eye split and are not — each takes one LED from each eye. That mistake is how 17_nondual_awareness_RGB ended up with its two banks cancelling by 25.8 dB at one sensor. Check your map before you trust a mask.

On the reference hardware, LED5 (mask 16) emits nothing measurable, which is why the measurement sessions park their timer-keepalive channel there. Bits above your populated channel count are not an error; they simply light nothing, and the linter notes it as LEDC_MASK_HIGH_BITS (info).

Above drive ~100 it is linear to about ±1 %. Below that it rolls off, reaching 68 % of linear at drive 12. This matters more than it sounds, because safe therapeutic sessions live exactly in that region — a dim amber at bright 20 on RGB 255 is drive 51.

The consequence that catches people is not the loss of absolute output; it is that the roll-off is uneven, so a brightness LFO in the low region is distorted in shape and not merely shallow. 17_nondual_awareness breathes ~8:22:12000 on blue 200 — effective drive 16 to 44 — so the dim end of its breath loses considerably more than the bright end and the curve is no longer a sine.

The fix is a change of variable, not a change of range: raise the RGB value and lower bright to hit the same light at a higher effective drive. RGB 255 at bright 24 and RGB 60 at bright 100 land within 2–3 % of each other in output, but only the first leaves you headroom to modulate linearly.

Colour

Colour does three jobs, and it is worth being clear which one you are using it for:

  • Drive strength. At matched luminance the spectral extremes drive hardest: amber/red ≈ 8.06 dB and blue/cyan ≈ 6.82 dB against green/lime's 2.85 dB. Any pair containing red or blue beats any pair without.
  • Eyes-closed transmission. Only warm light gets through the eyelid in useful quantity (~14.5 % at 700 nm versus ≤3 % below 580 nm). Eyes-closed sessions should bias amber/red at 620 nm or longer.
  • Mood and circadian framing. Warm amber for sleep, cool white or blue for focus. This has nothing to do with entrainment and everything to do with whether someone will use the session.

Safety constrains all three: no saturated-red flicker in the 3–60 Hz range (the usual test is R/(R+G+B) ≥ 0.8), and no red↔blue alternation at flicker rates. A warm amber such as 220,110,0 has a red fraction of 0.67 and passes; a steady red field is fine at any intensity, because the rule is about flicker.

All three colour columns are compound cells, so a colour can be ramped or modulated — a ~ on G walks a field from blue through white and back.

An absent env column on an LED line means 0 (square), and an out-of-range value also falls back to 0. env 4 has no meaning on the LED side and is treated as square. As on the audio side, duty is silently ignored by env 1 and env 2 — the sine and triangle carriers do not read it, and neither does a duty modulation placed next to them.

A square flicker injects its own harmonics into the response — a 10 Hz square gate is also driving 30 Hz at a third of the fundamental. If you care about which rate you are driving, env 1 is the clean choice: its third harmonic measures 0.004 against a square's 0.346. Both sine gates have been fixed to true raised sines; a measured h3 near 0.145 means the lookup table has regressed to the old parabola.

attack is only read by env 3. It defaults to 3 ms on a fresh channel, is click-safe at 2 ms and up, and is capped at 60 ms on the stepped path. The rendering path additionally clamps the effective edge to half the on-time, so a short gate degrades to a triangle rather than misbehaving. If you want an edge longer than 60 ms, what you actually want is env 1.

phase is a LEAD, not a lag

The firmware adds phase × period / 360 to elapsed time, so a non-zero phase makes the edge arrive earlier. It follows the sin(ωt + φ) convention, not “start this many degrees later”.

To delay an edge by N degrees, write 360 − N. 0 and 180 are symmetric under negation, so they are identical under either convention — and across the whole shipped library the phase column takes only those two values, 265 occurrences of 0 and 210 of 180. That is why the convention has never bitten anyone in practice, and why it is written down here before someone reaches for 90.

LED phase is applied at use-time from the live period, so it stays a fixed phase through a frequency ramp. Audio phase is stored as a fraction of the mod cycle and is frequency-independent by construction, which is the same behaviour reached a different way.

The antiphase pair: flicker you can barely see

Run two complementary-colour banks at the same rate, 180° apart, at matched luminance. The sum is nearly constant, so the field does not visibly flicker — while each retinal location still receives a full oscillation.

The amber bank at phase 0, the blue bank at phase 180, and their sum: a straight line. The LED carrier shapes are deliberately luminance-linear with no perceptual gamma applied, specifically so that a 180°-antiphase pair of raised sines adds up flat. Verified on hardware on the hardest shipped case: the fundamental cancelled by 25.8 dB at a sensor seeing both banks equally, at a relative phase of 178.1° against a commanded 180 — flat to 1.9°, which at 10 Hz is 0.53 ms.

#  time  freq  duty  bright   R    G   B  mask  env  phase
       0    40    50      20  255  140   0    10    0      0   # AMBER (warm bank), in phase
       0    40    50      20    0  120 255     5    0    180   # BLUE  (cool bank), antiphase

Three conditions have to hold or you get two dim flickering zones instead of one invisible field:

  1. The two banks must be physically interleaved in the optics, so each patch of retina sees both. That is a CONFIG_LED_CHANNEL_MAP question — e.g. an 8-pixel "0,1,2,3,0,1,2,3" interleave — not a .ledc question.
  2. The luminances must match. Equal bright with unequal RGB sums is not a match; amber 255,140,0 and blue 0,120,255 are close but your optics and LEDs have the final say.
  3. Both rows must share a timestamp, so the pair starts phase-coherent.

If you are going to measure an antiphase session, use one sensor per bank. A photocell that sees both measures the residual of a near-cancellation: on the verified case above the second harmonic sat 21.5 dB above the fundamental on that channel, and per-cycle analysis declined to grade about a third of the cycles. That is the design working, not a fault.

Jitter, and what it does differently from audio

LED jitter is written in the twelfth column as amp or amp:period_ms, and it is per channel-mask and wanders the flicker rate — a bipolar triangle LFO around the commanded rate. Amplitude is clamped to 5 Hz, and a period below 1000 ms is silently ignored (the previous period stays). The default period is 45 000 ms.

That is a genuinely different behaviour from the audio jitter column, which is global and moves only a binaural detune. If you want a wandering pulse rate, LED jitter is the one that does it. Write it back to 0 at the end of your session; the globals are not reset between sessions. A jittered channel is also excluded from the cycle-lattice re-anchor, by design — there is no fixed lattice to snap to.

Locking light to audio

Write the same rate in both places, 1:1. The LED line's freq and the audio beat or mod rate are independent numbers that you keep equal by hand; there is no follow mechanism.

A       420000   374    0   55    0   1    380     0            # audio beat 6 Hz
       420000     6   50   24  255  160    32    15    1    0   # LED 6 Hz, sine gate

A glide must carry its own > on each line, or the two drift apart across the ramp. Measured audio-versus-light offset on this hardware is a constant latency of about 2–3 ms with the audio early, holding across 2, 4 and 8 Hz (a constant-latency fit closed to 0.03 ms while a constant-phase fit needed 39.9° of spread, so it is unambiguously a latency). Cross-modal simultaneity tolerance is 20–40 ms, so none of this is a parameter you need to manage — the device's microsecond capability is margin. Spend the effort on envelopes and safety instead.

Two further pieces of received wisdom worth following: default the audio-versus-light phase offset to 0°, because no optimal offset has been demonstrated; and fade a flicker out at a trough rather than at a bright peak, which disengages more cleanly.

Rate limits and what happens outside them

The stepped path rejects freq > 100, duty > 100 and bright > 100. An out-of-range entry is dropped, not clamped: the install returns an error, the error is logged, and those channels get neither the new settings nor a fallback — they simply keep doing whatever they were doing. The sweep path used to validate nothing at all, so the identical values executed unchecked whenever the entry carried a ramp; the same three guards now apply there too, checking both endpoints of every ramp.

One hole remains: duty and bright are cast to uint8_t at parse time, before any guard sees them. 101–255 is caught, but above 255 the cast wraps — 300 becomes 44 — and the guard then sees a perfectly in-range value. Nothing warns. R, G and B are safe: they clamp and log.

Practically, 100 Hz is the ceiling and it is a bench number, not a therapeutic one — it is above flicker fusion. Rate accuracy is excellent on rates that divide the ISR tick evenly (40, 20, 16, 10, 8, 5, 4, 2, 1 Hz measured within ±0.01 %); rates that do not, like 7, 9 and 11 Hz, may be quantised by a few hundred ppm, and whether they are is an open question in the test suite.

Complete worked example

photic_antiphase_40hz.ledc — 15 minutes, a luminance-flat 40 Hz antiphase pair plus the 40 Hz click train. It reports no static problems found under python3 tools/avmeasure/analyze.py lint.

0 40 50 >0 255 140 0 10 0 0                  # AMBER bank, phase 0,   bright 0 -> 20
0 40 50 >0 0 120 255 5 0 180                 # BLUE  bank, phase 180, bright 0 -> 20
A 0 10000 0 >0 40 1 0 0 4 0 0 0 0 100        # ch1 GENUS click: 10 kHz carrier, 40 Hz square
                                             # gate, 4% duty, full depth. `attack` is written
                                             # 0 because env 0 ignores it; the sharp edge is
                                             # the point — GENUS specifies a 1 ms click train,
                                             # and 4% of a 25 ms period IS 1 ms.

15000 40 50 20 0 120 255 5 0 180             # blue matched to it => luminance ~flat

# --- Hold, fixed 40 Hz, ~14 min (00:30-14:30) ---
#   Nothing moves. GENUS efficacy depends on a FIXED 40 Hz: no frequency LFO and
#   no anti-habituation drift here, unlike the slower sessions.

What that file is doing deliberately:

  1. 40 Hz is above the 15–25 Hz danger band, which is the only reason a full-field flicker protocol is defensible at all. Every slower session in the library keeps the light at or below 10 Hz, or keeps it dim and single-eye, or drops the light entirely.
  2. Brightness is capped at 20 % and the duty 50 keeps a luminance floor, so there is no black-to-white contrast swing.
  3. Light ramps in over 15 s and out over 30 s. Never start or stop flicker abruptly; 10 s is the working minimum for light, 5 s for audio.
  4. The two banks share every timestamp, so they stay phase-coherent, and their brightness ramps are written identically so the luminance match holds through the fades.
  5. Nothing drifts during the hold. Anti-habituation jitter is right for a 16-minute theta drum and wrong here: the 40 Hz protocol's effect is specified at a fixed rate.
  6. Amber and blue, not red. No saturated-red strobe, no red↔blue alternation.

Photic safety checklist

Because there is no device-side clamp, this is a manual checklist and every item is a thing a shipped session actually does:

  • Avoid bright full-field flicker at 15–25 Hz. If the target rate is in that band, let audio carry the beat and leave the light out — and remember that freq 0 means off, so "out" means no LED line at all.
  • Between 3 and 60 Hz, cap brightness and ramp light in and out over at least 10 s. Never stop abruptly.
  • Keep a brightness floor — avoid full black↔white contrast. A 50 % duty with a non-zero dim level does this for free.
  • No saturated-red flicker (R/(R+G+B) ≥ 0.8) at any rate above 3 Hz, and no red↔blue alternation at flicker rates.
  • If you must use a danger-band rate — 30_splitfield_lucid runs one eye at 16 Hz — keep it dim and single-eye, never full-field bright, and document the intent in the header. Expect the linter and reviewers to warn, and let them.
  • Put the contraindication block in every session header: photosensitive epilepsy, seizure disorder, photic migraine; caution if pregnant or wearing an implant; never while driving; stop if dizzy or unwell; experiential, not medical.
