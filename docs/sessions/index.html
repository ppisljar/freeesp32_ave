Session developer

A session is one plain-text .ledc file: a timeline of timestamped rows that tell the device what tone to make and what light to flash, and how to glide between them. This section is for people writing and editing those files — what each modality actually is, what the evidence for it does and does not support, and how to express it in the format.

This is a DIY research instrument, not a medical device. Nothing here is therapy, diagnosis or clinical advice. Flickering light in the 3–30 Hz band is a genuine seizure trigger for people with photosensitive epilepsy, and the 15–25 Hz sub-band (peak 16–20 Hz) accounts for the great majority of photosensitive reactions. Do not use the device, and do not hand a session to anyone else, if there is any history of photosensitive epilepsy, a seizure disorder, or photic migraine. Read the safety section on the overview page before you author anything that flickers.

Where to start

The first four pages are the four ways this device can carry a rhythm. The fifth is everything you can then do with them — and it is the longer list.

  • Binaural beats — two carriers, one per ear; the beat is the difference and it is assembled in the listener, not in the signal. Headphones mandatory. Two idioms in this format, and the beat is now directly glidable.
  • Monaural beats — the same two carriers summed before the ear, so the beat is physically in the waveform. Works on a speaker. Much deeper modulation; the robust choice for waking states.
  • Isochronic tones — one carrier gated on and off. The depth column decides whether that is a true pulse or a gentle tremolo, and it is the single parameter that sets the effect size.
  • Photic / LED flicker — rate, duty, brightness, colour, zone masks, envelope shape and phase. The strongest driver on the device, and the one with real safety constraints.
  • Stimulus markers — announce experiment events to an EEG recorder with M lines. Three timestamps per marker (scheduled, dispatched, predicted onset) and three sinks: a TTL pulse, UDP into Lab Streaming Layer, and JSONL on the SD card.
  • Techniques beyond the four modalities — antiphase “invisible” flicker, EEG-contour carriers, AM noise, split-field, traveling waves, anti-habituation jitter, harmonic stacking, the breath layer, envelope shaping and colour. Also the honest list of what was researched and not built.
  • The complete .ledc reference — every line type, every column, every default, every range, in tables. This is the one-page lookup.
  • Authoring guide — session arc and pacing, safe ramp-in, how to test a session, what the static linter's findings mean, and the traps that have actually bitten people.
  • Session generator and simulator — the device's own editor, running in your browser with no hardware attached. Write or load a session and hear it: the audio comes from a port of the firmware's synthesis engine, the light preview is the exact byte sequence the LED strip would receive, and the inspector shows what every channel is doing at the playhead.

The one idea the whole format is built on

Every channel — audio or LED — has two layers, and almost every column belongs to one of them.

The carrier / pulse split. Source: ledc_format.md, “Model: two layers per channel”.
LayerWhat it isAn LED line expresses it asAn audio line expresses it as
Carrier the thing you actually see or hear R G B (colour) and bright freq, freqR, waveType (left pitch, right pitch, timbre)
Pulse the entrainment rhythm imposed on the carrier freq (flicker Hz), duty, env, phase, attack, jitter mod (Hz), duty, env, phase, attack, jitter, depth

The one naming trap. The entrainment rate is freq on an LED line but mod on an audio line — freq on an audio line is the carrier pitch, which is a completely different quantity. Read every row's line type before you read its numbers.

One asymmetry is worth internalising now because it explains a whole class of “why is my isochronic session so weak” questions: an LED pulse is always full depth — the envelope reaches zero between flashes by construction — while an audio pulse has a depth, and until recently that depth was an invisible hardcoded 10 %. See isochronic tones.

The four kinds of line

Line types, from parse_line in main/config_parser.c.
ShapeWhat it does
<time> <freq> <duty> <bright> <R> <G> <B> <mask> [env] [phase] [attack] [jitter] LED. 8 to 12 tokens. A 5-token legacy form also exists.
A <time> <freq> <pan> <vol> <mod> [ch] [freqR] [waveType] [duty] [env] [phase] [attack] [jitter] [depth] Audio. 5 or more tokens after the A.
BG <url> <pan> <loudness> A background audio bed for the whole session. Not a timeline entry.
S <time> <voice> <volume> "<text>" A spoken cue.

# starts a comment, inline or whole-line, and blank lines are ignored. The full grammar, with every token count, limit and fallback, is on the reference page.

Choosing a modality

headphones Binaural

Gentlest. Effective modulation is only around 3 dB, because the beat is a perceptual artifact rather than a signal. Needs stereo separation, so headphones are not optional. Good for sleep and low-arousal content.

speaker ok Monaural

The same two carriers, both centre-panned, so the beat forms acoustically. Near-100 % envelope modulation. One speaker or one earbud carries it. The robust default for waking states.

speaker ok Isochronic

One carrier gated on and off. Sharpest attack and the most adjustable: depth, duty and edge shape are all yours. Also the easiest to make click.

eyes Photic

Flickering light. The strongest driver of a steady-state response by a wide margin, and the only modality with a real safety envelope. Combines additively with audio at the same rate.

Bands, and how much to believe about them

The band-to-state mappings below are the device-and-practitioner convention the shipped session library is built around. They are not settled neuroscience. What is reasonably well established is narrower: that periodic sensory stimulation produces a measurable steady-state response at the stimulus rate, most reliably in the alpha region and at 40 Hz; that combined audio-plus-visual stimulation produces a stronger steady-state response than either alone; and that the response needs a slow glide to follow a changing rate. The leap from “there is a steady-state response at 6 Hz” to “the listener is now in a theta state with theta-state properties” is exactly the leap the evidence does not make. Author accordingly, and say so in your session headers — every shipped session does.
