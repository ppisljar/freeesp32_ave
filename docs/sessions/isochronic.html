Isochronic tones

One carrier, switched on and off at the entrainment rate. No second carrier, no interference, no perceptual assembly — just a gate. The sharpest and most adjustable audio stimulus the device makes, and the one where a single column decides whether you get a pulse or a barely-audible wobble.

What the phenomenon is

Take any carrier — a 250 Hz sine, a 10 kHz click train, a band of pink noise — and multiply it by a periodic gate. The result is a train of discrete bursts at the gate rate. Three parameters describe the gate completely: how fast it repeats (mod), how much of each cycle it is up (duty), and how far down it pulls the carrier (depth). A fourth, the edge shape, is set by env and attack.

Upper lane, the gate. The solid trace is depth 100 — it falls all the way to zero between pulses, so the carrier reaches real silence and the result is a true isochronic pulse. The dashed trace is depth 40: it never drops below 60 % of full, so the carrier is always audible and what you hear is a tremolo, a loudness wobble on a continuous tone, not a pulse train. The raised section occupies 40 % of each period, which is duty 40, and its edges are sloped by attack. Lower lane, the carrier multiplied by the full-depth gate. Note that duty and depth are orthogonal: duty moves the edges in time, depth moves the floor in amplitude.

Mechanism, as currently understood

Of the three audio modalities this is the one whose mechanism needs the least hedging. A gated carrier contains an unambiguous amplitude modulation at the gate rate; the auditory periphery transduces it; the auditory steady-state response at that rate is large and easy to measure. There is nothing perceptual or inferential about the stimulus itself, unlike a binaural beat.

Beyond that, the honest picture is the same as everywhere else in this field. The strongest result in the entrainment literature is the 40 Hz gamma work — a 40 Hz light flicker combined with a 40 Hz click train, which is an isochronic tone, delivered for an hour a day. The entrainment in that protocol is solidly established; the disease-modifying claims built on top of it are contested and the human trials are early. For the slower bands the position is weaker still: the steady-state response is real and measurable, and the claim that producing it reconfigures your subjective state in the way band-to-state tables promise is practitioner convention with thin and mixed support.

There is one mechanistic detail from the entrainment literature that does change how you author: what appears to drive the response is the attack steepness and the off-time, not the hold or the decay. That argues for shorter duties — more off-time — and for keeping the rising edge reasonably crisp. The commonly quoted working range is a duty between 25 and 50 %, with an attack of a few milliseconds, and depth 0.7–1.0 for a frank isochronic pulse versus 0.1–0.3 for a modulation deliberately disguised inside music.

What it is typically used for

  • Waking states — focus, SMR, energise. The strongest audio drive available, and you can keep the beat entirely in audio when photic flicker in the 15–25 Hz band would be unsafe. 05_focus_smr.ledc carries its 14 Hz SMR beat 100 % on an audio isochronic for exactly that reason.
  • The 40 Hz gamma protocol. 07_genus_40hz.ledc implements the published click train faithfully: a 10 kHz carrier gated at 40 Hz with a 4 % duty, which at a 25 ms period is a 1 ms click, square-gated, held fixed for an hour.
  • Amplitude-modulated noise beds. Gating pink or brown noise gives a broad cochlear drive with low listening fatigue, which suits sleep content. 28_noise_sleep_bed.ledc uses a smooth 0.85 Hz sine gate; 29_noise_sleep_pulsed.ledc uses a trapezoid gate at the same rate with a 40 % duty and a 50 ms edge.
  • Percussive reinforcement. A low-carrier isochronic layered under a monaural drum adds a felt thump, as in 36_shamanic_trance_drum.ledc.

Rate-band safety. An isochronic tone is safe at any rate — it is light, not sound, that triggers photosensitive seizures. But isochronic sessions are usually locked 1:1 to a flickering LED, and that light is not safe at every rate. Any flicker in the 3–30 Hz band is a genuine seizure trigger for susceptible people, worst between 15 and 25 Hz. If your target rate is in that band, the right answer is usually to let the audio carry the beat and keep the light out of it. See safety. This is a DIY research instrument, not a medical device.

How to configure it in .ledc

An isochronic channel is a single audio line with a non-zero mod:

#         time  freq  pan  vol  mod  ch  freqR  wave  duty  env  phase  attack  jitter  depth
A            0   250    0   70   10   1      0     0    40    3      0       5       0    100

That is a 250 Hz sine on channel 1, gated at 10 Hz, on for 40 % of each cycle, trapezoid-shaped with 5 ms edges, at full depth.

The depth column, which is the whole game

depth is the fourteenth and last audio column, a compound cell clamped to 0–100 %. It is how far the gate pulls the carrier down:

unipolar gates (env 0..3):   sample *= (1 - depth) + depth * gate
bipolar tremolo (env 4):     sample *= 1 + depth * sin(mod_phase)

So on a gate, 100 means the carrier reaches silence between pulses — a true isochronic tone — and 0 means no modulation at all. On env 4 it is the classic tremolo index.

Why this column exists, and why old sessions sound weak. The depth used to be a hardcoded 0.1 with no column at all and no mention in the format spec. Every “isochronic” gate in every session therefore swung the amplitude only between 0.9 and 1.0 of its carrier — an AM index of about 5 %, which is a barely-perceptible tremolo rather than discrete pulses. It was the single parameter that sets isochronic effect size, and it was invisible. Several shipped session headers still carry a note along the lines of “engine note: AM depth is fixed (~0.1) here, so the trapezoid pulse is a gentle buzz, not a hard on/off”. Those notes are now historical; the column exists. Older files are untouched by design — see the default table below.

Full depth is quieter — raise vol

A 50 %-duty gate at depth 100 is −3.01 dB in mean power against the same tone ungated. That is not a bug or a scaling artifact: the carrier really is off half the time. A 40 % duty costs about −4 dB, and the 4 % GENUS click costs about −14 dB. Perceived loudness drops with the power, so vol usually needs raising when you turn depth up. At the old 10 % depth the difference was around 0.2 dB, which is why nobody ever noticed it before.

There is room to raise it. The mixer divides every channel by a fixed 1/16 regardless of how many are playing, so a single centre-panned sine at vol 100 peaks at only −27.1 dBFS, and vol is not range-checked on any session path — a value above 100 works and is simply louder. Use that deliberately and sparingly; see the mixing model.

Full depth on a square gate clicks — use env 3

A square gate at full depth puts a step discontinuity in the waveform at every edge, four times a second at 2 Hz and eighty times a second at 40 Hz. That is a click by construction, and at full depth it is loud.

The click-free route to full depth is env 3 (trapezoid) with attack 3–10 ms, which rounds both edges. Two helpful details: the edge is clamped to half the on-time, so a very short gate degrades gracefully into a triangle rather than misbehaving; and audio attack has no upper cap, unlike the LED side's 60 ms. Anything at or above about 2 ms is click-safe. The default on a fresh channel is 3 ms.

If you want the click — the 40 Hz protocol specifies a 1 ms click train, and a click is what it means — then env 0 with a 4 % duty is correct, and attack is simply ignored there. Write it as 0 so the file does not imply an edge shaping that never happens.

When the env column is absent an audio line defaults to 4, the legacy tremolo — note that an LED line defaults to 0 instead. An out-of-range value also falls back to 4 on an audio line and to 0 on an LED line.

duty is silently ignored by env 1 and env 2. The sine gate returns 0.5 − 0.5·cos(2πph) and the triangle returns a fixed symmetric ramp; neither function reads duty at all. So a duty column next to env 1 or env 2 is a no-op, and a duty modulation next to them is a no-op too — the modulation engine dutifully animates a value that nothing reads. The triangle's on-fraction is always exactly 50 %. If duty matters, use env 0 or env 3.

Related curiosity, so you do not chase it: env 1 measures an apparent duty of 66.67 %. That is just the fraction of a raised sine that sits above its own mid-level, not a setting being ignored.

Both sine envelopes are now true raised sines, not the 4x(1−x) parabola their comments once advertised. The parabola looks like a similar hump but has a derivative discontinuity at the cycle boundary, so its harmonics fell as 1/n²: an 8 Hz alpha gate was simultaneously driving 16 Hz at a quarter of the fundamental and 24 Hz at a ninth — beta content inside an alpha session. Measured second harmonic went from 0.251 to 0.002 when it was fixed. If you measure h2 near 0.25 on an env 1 channel, the lookup table has regressed.

Gliding the rate, duty and depth

mod, duty, attack, phase and depth are all compound cells. mod takes every glyph; depth takes > and * only — a periodic glyph on it logs a warning and holds the start value.

A       120000   250    0   70  >10   1      0     0    40    3      0       5       0    >60
A       420000   250    0   70    6   1      0     0    40    3      0       5       0    100

That glides the pulse rate 10 → 6 Hz and simultaneously deepens the gate from 60 % to 100 % over five minutes. env is not compound: it is a step value, and a glyph on it is parsed and silently discarded by design.

Two start-value rules differ by field class, and they matter here:

  • Core fields (freq, pan, vol, mod): a ramp on an already-running channel starts from the live value and discards the literal in the file. That is deliberate — starting from the literal snaps audibly if the previous ramp was interrupted — but it means a literal that disagrees with the live state is lying to its reader. Write the live value; the linter flags the mismatch as LEDC_RAMP_START_SUBSTITUTED.
  • Pulse fields (duty, phase, attack, freqR, depth): the start value is the literal, and the target is found by scanning forward for the next entry that actually sets that field — so an intervening row that omits the column does not break the ramp.

A stepped duty 0 is a no-op

The setter only writes duty > 0, so writing duty 0 leaves the previous duty in force. The linter reports it as LEDC_AUDIO_DUTY_ZERO (info). A ramped or modulated duty can reach 0, because the per-block evaluation takes a different path. To silence a channel entirely, use vol 0; to remove the modulation and keep the tone, use mod 0.

Locking light to the pulse

Write the same rate in the LED line's freq column, and match the gate character:

A            0   250    0   70   10   1      0     0    40    3      0       5       0    100
             0    10   40   25  255  170    40    15    3      0       6      # LED 10 Hz, env 3

Audio and LED env values mean the same shapes, which makes 1:1 locking easy to keep coherent — a trapezoid gate on both sides, with similar edge fractions, reads as one event rather than two. Measured audio-versus-light offset is a constant ~2–3 ms with audio early, far below the 20–40 ms cross-modal simultaneity threshold.

Audio phase is stored as a fraction of the mod cycle, so it is frequency-independent by construction and survives a mod ramp. It is also, like LED phase, a lead — see the photic page.

Complete worked example

isochronic_alpha_pulse.ledc — 15 minutes at 10 Hz, full-depth trapezoid gate, light locked 1:1. It reports no static problems found under python3 tools/avmeasure/analyze.py lint.

# --- Induction (00:00-01:00): light and tone ease up together at 10 Hz ---
0 10 40 >0 255 170 40 15 3 0 6                 # amber trapezoid flash, 40% duty, 6 ms edge
A 0 250 0 >0 10 1 0 0 40 3 0 5 0 100           # ch1 250 Hz gated at 10 Hz, duty 40, FULL depth
15000 10 40 25 255 170 40 15 3 0 6             # brightness reaches the 25% cap
A 60000 250 0 70 10 1 0 0 40 3 0 5 0 100       # vol settled 70 (raised to pay for the gate)

# --- Hold, 10 Hz alpha, 12 min (01:00-13:00) ---
#   Anti-habituation lives on the LIGHT here: `jitter 0.3:53000` wanders the
#   flicker rate +/-0.3 Hz over 53 s. Audio `jitter` would be useless on this
#   channel — it only ever moves a binaural detune, and this channel is mono.
#   To wander an isochronic pulse rate, modulate `mod` instead: ~9.7:10.3:53000.
A 120000 250 0 70 10 1 0 0 40 3 0 5 0 100
120000 10 40 25 255 170 40 15 3 0 6 0.3:53000

# --- Close (13:00-15:00): simultaneous fade of light and tone ---
A 780000 250 0 >70 10 1 0 0 40 3 0 5 0 100     # volume fades 70 -> 0 over 2 min
780000 10 40 >25 255 170 40 15 3 0 6 0         # LED jitter back off, brightness 25 -> 0
A 900000 250 0 0 10 1 0 0 40 3 0 5 0 100
900000 10 40 0 255 170 40 15 3 0 6 0

Why that file looks the way it does:

  1. depth 100 is written out on every row, even though env 3 would default to it. The file states its own intent.
  2. vol 70, not 50. A 40 %-duty full-depth gate costs about 4 dB of mean power.
  3. env 3 with attack 5 rather than env 0: full depth plus a square edge would click eighty times a minute at 10 Hz.
  4. Anti-habituation is on the light, not the audio. Audio jitter on a mono channel does nothing, and the file says so in a comment rather than quietly carrying a dead column.
  5. Jitter is written back to 0 before the end, because the LED jitter globals are not reset between sessions.
