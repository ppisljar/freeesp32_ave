Speech and narration

Some sessions talk to you — a line at the start, a cue at a transition, a word at the end. The thing to understand before any of the buttons make sense is that the device cannot speak. It has no speech synthesiser. It plays audio files. Everything below is about getting those files made and onto the card.

Who actually does the talking

A session asks for narration with an S line — a timestamp and some text. Nothing in that line is audio; it is a request for a phrase.

Your browser synthesises it, not the device. When you press play with the web UI open, the browser turns each phrase into audio and mixes it in. That works, but it means the laptop has to stay open and online — which is exactly what you do not want when the point is to lie down with the glasses on.

So there is a second path: synthesise every phrase once, upload the resulting files to the device's SD card, and from then on the device plays them itself with nothing attached. That is what the two buttons in Settings do, and the rest of this page is about them.

The short version. Settings → Browser preferences: pick a Speech (TTS) engine, press ⤓ Preload all speech, then press ⇪ Sync speech to SD card. Do it once, redo it when you add or edit a talking session.

Choosing an engine

Settings → Browser preferences → Speech (TTS) engine. There are two, both free, neither needing an API key you have to find and paste somewhere.

The engine choice lives in your browser, not on the device — and it changes the voice. Two consequences people trip over: a different laptop means a different setting and a different narrator, and switching engine invalidates everything you already generated, because the files are named after the voice as well as the words. Decide first, then preload.

The Puter sign-in, and why you are being asked

The first time Puter synthesises something it may open a sign-in window. This surprises people, so: Puter is a cloud platform whose AI services run on a user-pays model. Instead of this project shipping an API key and paying for everyone's speech, the usage is attributed to your Puter account. That is the trade: no key to configure, but an account to create.

Creating one is free and takes a moment — the popup walks you through it, or you can sign up at puter.com first and simply log in when asked. Once you have signed in, the browser remembers it; you will not be asked again on that browser.

If no popup appears and nothing happens, your browser blocked it. The sign-in has to open from a real click, which is why it fires the moment you press the button rather than later in the process — allow popups for the device's address and press the button again.

If you would rather not create an account at all, switch to the Google engine. It needs no sign-in; it just needs the device to have internet.

Step 1 — Preload all speech

⤓ Preload all speech walks the S lines of every session it can see — the ones on the device and the ones saved in this browser — collects every unique phrase, synthesises the ones it does not already have, and stores them in this browser.

The status line counts as it goes and tells you at the end how many were generated versus already cached. Phrases already in the cache cost nothing and make no network request, so running it again after adding one session is quick.

After this, the browser can play and bounce speech with no internet — which is the useful state to be in before you leave the house. It does not yet mean the device can speak on its own; that is the next step.

Step 2 — Sync speech to SD card

⇪ Sync speech to SD card uploads the phrases to the device, so a session can run with the browser closed and the laptop shut.

It checks the card is there, reports how much space is free, then uploads only what is missing — phrases already on the card are skipped, and phrases already in the browser cache upload without being synthesised again. A full re-run after a small change moves very little.

Each unique phrase becomes one file under /sdcard/speech/, named from a hash of the voice and the text rather than from the words themselves. You do not need to care what they are called, but it explains two behaviours:

  • Editing a phrase creates a new file rather than replacing one. Change a comma and you have a new name; the old file stays on the card until you delete it. Harmless, but the card accumulates.
  • Changing the engine renames everything, because the voice is part of the name. Re-sync after switching.

If a phrase is missing from the card, the session does not fail — it simply falls back to the browser for that line, as long as a browser is driving playback. Offline, on the device alone, a missing phrase is silence where the narration should have been.

When it does not talk

SymptomUsually
Silent where narration should be, playing from the card The phrase was never synced — or was edited after the last sync. Run ⇪ Sync speech to SD card again.
Silent after changing the voice or engine Different engine, different filenames. Preload and sync again.
Synthesis fails with the browser open Puter needs internet in the browser and may need its sign-in popup allowed; Google needs the device online.
Nothing works while on the device's own access point Expected. ESP32-AVE-Setup has no internet, so neither engine can reach anything — preload before you go off-network.
No sync button effect, "no SD card detected" The card is not mounted. See offline playback.

More failure modes, including the ones that are not about speech, are on the troubleshooting page.
