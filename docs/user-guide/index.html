User guide

You have a working device. This section is about using it: getting to its web page, starting a session, understanding what every slider actually does to the experience, and what to do when something looks broken.

Flicker can trigger seizures. This device drives rhythmic light, and the rates it is designed around — the Live Control flicker slider runs 0–30 Hz — sit squarely across the 3–30 Hz band that is a genuine seizure trigger for people with photosensitive epilepsy; the risk peaks around 15–25 Hz. Do not use it if you have any history of seizures. Take care if you are pregnant or have a pacemaker or other implant, never use it while driving or operating machinery, and stop immediately if you feel dizzy, disoriented or unwell. Read the safety section on the overview page before your first session.
This is a DIY research and meditation instrument, not a medical device. Nothing here is certified, approved or clinically validated, nothing it does is a treatment for anything, and the firmware contains no therapeutic safety interlock — the one planned brightness/depth clamp for the 15–25 Hz band was deliberately not built. The only safety gate that ships is a one-time notice in the browser that you click through. You are the safety system.

The thirty-second version

1. Reach the device

Browse to http://esp32-ave.local. If it has never been configured, join its own WiFi network ESP32-AVE-Setup and go to http://192.168.4.1 instead.

2. Pick a session

On the Home tab, choose a file from the Configs dropdown. It loads into the text box so you can see exactly what will run.

3. Press play

▶ PLAY Config with a Delay of 60 s gives you a minute to put the glasses and headphones on before anything happens.

4. Stop whenever

The red ■ STOP button in the top bar is always visible on every tab and kills all audio and light at once. Holding the device's hardware button for three seconds does the same thing.

What this guide does not cover

Writing or editing sessions — the Generator tab, the .ledc timeline format, and the Text / Table / Lanes / Wizard editors — is its own subject. See the session developer section. Building, wiring and flashing a device for the first time is in hardware.
